# OVERTURE — Brand OS
**Pathway B (Endorsed) · Naming Alternative Tied to the Launch Moment**

---

## 1. BLUF

OVERTURE is the introductory piece that sets the tone for everything that follows — the natural name for a studio arm defining itself through its own launch sequence, from the October 2026 NYC Studio Launch through the PRELUDE demo mission. It wins on structural timeliness and loses on durability: an overture is, by definition, an opening act, and a brand built entirely around "the beginning" needs a deliberate second act once the beginning is over.

**Positioning:** OVERTURE is the opening movement of a new era of orbital media — the first piece, setting the tone for everything the platform will become.

---

## 2. Brand Narrative & World

An overture is not incidental to a performance — in classical and operatic tradition, it's often where a composer states the themes the entire work will develop, compressed into a single introductory piece the audience hears before anything else. OVERTURE claims that role literally: it is the Studio Arm's first public statement, timed precisely to the platform's own opening sequence (NYC Studio Launch, PRELUDE, the first commercial pilots).

Being an OVERTURE client feels like being part of the founding moment — not just an early adopter, but present at the literal opening statement of a new medium. This narrative works hardest in the specific window between now and the PRELUDE demo mission; its emotional power is front-loaded by design, which is both its greatest strength as a launch brand and its central long-term liability.

**The hook:** founding-moment prestige. Being early to something genuinely new is a distinct status claim from being permanent (Monolith) or being first-in-signal (Spectra) — it's the appeal of being present at an origin story, which is a real and durable form of prestige for clients who want their own brand history to include "we were there when this started."

## 3. The 5-Subsystem Brand OS

### Core Bus (Conductor) → Legal & Entity Isolation
Full legal and operational separation, identical to every posture. OVERTURE's collision exposure is thematic rather than mechanical — it doesn't map to a named platform component the way Cadenza (Virtuoso) does, but it maps directly and publicly onto the **PRELUDE** demo mission itself, which is a named, dated, externally referenced program. A client asking "overture to what?" has a documented, searchable answer within one search query.

### Interface Protocol (SWIS) → Plug-and-Play Onboarding
Framed as "the opening statement" — a client's 15 kg allocation becomes part of the platform's literal founding narrative. Onboarding language should lean into being first, being present, being part of the record — a client isn't just integrating a payload, they're becoming part of how this medium's origin story gets told later.

### Power Engine (Dynamic PMS) → Moments-Over-Mass Pricing
OVERTURE sells **Statements** — pricing tied explicitly to the platform's launch calendar rather than a generic campaign cycle. An **Opening Bar** (a short capture window during the NYC Studio Launch period itself, priced as the single most historically significant — and most heavily discounted, as a founding-member incentive — booking available), a **First Movement** (the flagship PRELUDE-era engagement), and a **Standing Ovation** (a rare, single retrospective booking marking the transition from PRELUDE to Adagio-01, sold only to founding OVERTURE clients as a loyalty moment). This pricing structure is more calendar-bound than any other brand's, which is a deliberate feature during launch and a structural challenge afterward — see Section 7.

### Attitude Control (AOCS) → Dynamic Brand Voice
With investors and technical partners, OVERTURE's register should lean into "founding" and "first" language more explicitly than any other posture — this is the one brand where investor-facing and client-facing voice can converge more closely, since the founding-moment narrative is genuinely useful in both contexts. With creative clients, the emphasis shifts toward legacy-in-the-making rather than the technical proof points.

### Actuator (Harmony Robotics) → Creative Actuation & Media Rights
The robotic swap between clients during the launch window is narrated as **turning the page** — each client's Statement becoming part of a continuous, published record of the platform's opening chapter. Full media rights bundle into the booking, plus OVERTURE-specific participation in the platform's own founding-moment documentary content (a genuine crossover opportunity with the Deep-Tech Tenant Storytelling segment described in `market/segmentation.md`).

## 4. Verbal Identity

**Naming rationale:** An overture is the piece that comes first and sets the tone for the whole performance — it maps precisely onto the Studio Arm's actual current moment (pre-launch, building toward PRELUDE) in a way none of the other four names do. That precision is also its expiration date: the metaphor stops making structural sense once the platform is no longer new.

**Taglines:**
- **Evolutionary:** "The first movement of a new era."
- **Disruptive:** "Before the performance, there's the statement. This is it."
- **Category-Breaking:** "History doesn't start at the headline. It starts at the overture — and this is where you're written into it."

**Tone words:** foundational, anticipatory, historic, first, deliberate, resonant, opening.

**We say / we never say:**
- We say "statement" / we never say "campaign" (the whole brand promise is founding-moment significance, not a standard media buy).
- We say "founding" / we never say "early access" (early access is a SaaS term; founding is a cultural-history term — the register matters).
- We say "the record" / we never say "the archive" (archive implies something closed and past; record implies something still being written).

## 5. Visual Identity Direction

**Palette logic:** theater-and-playbill vocabulary — the specific visual world of the moment just before a curtain rises, not the performance itself. Curtain-red and proscenium-gilt against a parchment ground, distinct from Aria's spotlight-on-dark-stage palette.

| Swatch | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Curtain Charcoal | `#191B1E` | Ground — the darkness just before the curtain rises, not the performance itself. |
| Parchment | `#EFE8D8` | Ground — playbill and program paper, warmer and more textured than the other brands' off-whites. |
| Curtain Crimson | `#A33B34` | Primary accent — the specific red of a theater curtain, used only on the founding/launch-specific product tier (Opening Bar). |
| Proscenium Brass | `#9C7A3C` | Secondary — gilt detailing, used for supporting structural elements and the OVERTURE wordmark itself. |

**Typographic attitude:** a classic playbill-style serif for headlines — more overtly theatrical and ornamental than any other brand in the system, appropriate to OVERTURE's explicitly ceremonial, occasion-driven positioning.

**Motion/photography feel:** anticipatory framing — compositions that suggest a "just before" moment (a curtain edge, a held breath before a downbeat) rather than the performance's climax, which every other brand in the system claims for itself.

**Logotype concept:** a single rising curtain-edge shape, rendered as clean geometry rather than a literal curtain illustration — evokes "about to begin" without becoming a theater-prop cliché.

## 6. Governance Mapping

- **Mass Silence Rule:** Statement/Opening Bar/First Movement vocabulary fully replaces engineering language client-side.
- **40% Capacity Cap:** First Movement engagements count against the anchor cap; Opening Bar and Standing Ovation bookings sell spot/flex, with Opening Bar specifically discounted as a founding-member incentive rather than a margin-capture premium — a deliberate, time-boxed exception to the standard spot/flex pricing logic that should sunset once the launch window closes.
- **Data Plane Firewall:** Framed as "your statement is recorded on its own line" — identical DLT-based isolation.
- **GC Veto Gate:** Every Statement clears GC screening before booking, with particular attention during the launch window to ensure founding clients' public "we were there first" marketing doesn't inadvertently reference platform specifics that should stay under Mass Silence.
- **Conversion Engine:** A PRELUDE-era OVERTURE client receives an Adagio Conversion Credit toward a standing engagement — narrated as earning a place in the "Standing Ovation" retrospective booking once Adagio-01 is operational, which doubles as OVERTURE's built-in conversion incentive.

## 7. Naming Clearance + Red-Team

**Trademark landmine scan:** "Overture" is a well-known former name in ad-tech — Overture Services, the pioneering pay-per-click search advertising company acquired by Yahoo in 2003. While the brand has been dormant for two decades, it remains a documented, relevant-class collision given that advertising/media is exactly the Studio Arm's target category, and any experienced ad-industry buyer or agency partner (a newly emphasized segment per `market/segmentation.md`) is more likely than average to recognize the name. Broader secondary use exists in finance (deal terminology — "overture" as a preliminary offer) and consumer software.

**Cultural-collision scan:** Carries the same structural Brand Collision exposure as every Pathway B name — proximity to the Symphony family's musical register — plus a unique, additional exposure specific to OVERTURE: it points directly and publicly at the **PRELUDE** demo mission, which is itself a named, dated, externally documented program in Symphony's own investor materials. This is a different kind of risk than Aria's or Cadenza's internal-hardware links — PRELUDE is meant to become public knowledge as the mission approaches, meaning OVERTURE's collision exposure is scheduled to *increase* over time as PRELUDE gets more press coverage, not remain static.

**Recommendation: Reconsider for a durable brand; viable only as a deliberately time-boxed launch identity.** OVERTURE's structural weakness isn't collision risk alone — it's that the metaphor has a built-in expiration date tied to the platform's own maturity. If pursued, it should be scoped explicitly as a pre-PRELUDE launch brand with a planned transition to one of the other four names (most likely Rhapsody or Spectra) once the "opening" narrative stops matching the platform's actual maturity — not adopted as the Studio Arm's permanent identity.
